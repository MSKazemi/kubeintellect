# Contributions are not gated on anything you have to sign

**KubeIntellect does not require a DCO sign-off, and there is no CLA.** You do not need to add
a `Signed-off-by` line, run `git commit -s`, tick a box, create an account, or get anything
reviewed by an employer's legal team. Opening the pull request is the whole contract, and
nothing checks for a sign-off, so there is nothing you can get wrong.

## How your contribution is licensed, then

Your contribution is under **AGPL-3.0-or-later** — the same licence as the rest of the
project — and **you keep the copyright in your own work.**

Two things establish that without asking you to sign anything:

1. **GitHub's Terms of Service** provide the inbound licence for public repositories: a
   contribution to a repository that carries a licence notice is licensed under those same
   terms. That covers every pull request opened here.
2. **Submitting to a project is understood to license under that project's terms.** Knowingly
   contributing to an AGPL-3.0 repository is submitting under AGPL-3.0. This is long-standing
   practice across the free-software ecosystem.

Being straight with you about the trade-off: unlike Apache-2.0, the GPL family does **not**
write an inbound-contribution clause into the licence text. That is the reason large copyleft
projects such as the Linux kernel adopted a DCO in the first place. We have decided the
certainty it adds is not worth turning a newcomer's first pull request red — but that is a
judgement call, not a technicality, and it is recorded here rather than hidden.

**The commercial licence offered elsewhere covers the copyright holder's own code.** It does
not reach your contribution, and nothing here asks you to let it.

If you *prefer* to sign off your commits out of habit, that is welcome and changes nothing.
